# German Festivals and Holidays (Feste und Feiertage)

## Oktoberfest (Munich)
- The world's largest folk festival (Volksfest), held every year in Munich, Bavaria, on the Theresienwiese.
- It started on 12 October 1810 with the wedding of Crown Prince Ludwig (later King Ludwig I) and Princess Therese of Saxe-Hildburghausen. The citizens of Munich were invited to celebrate, and the field was named "Theresienwiese" (Therese's meadow) after the bride.
- Despite its name, it begins in mid or late September and ends on the first Sunday of October (or on 3 October if that is later). It lasts 16 to 18 days.
- It opens when the Mayor of Munich taps the first barrel of beer and shouts "O'zapft is!" (Bavarian for "It's tapped!").
- Only beer from Munich breweries may be served in the big tents. Beer is served in a one-litre glass called "die Maß".
- Traditional clothing: Dirndl (dress for women) and Lederhosen (leather trousers for men).
- About six million visitors come every year.

## Christmas (Weihnachten)
- The Advent season (die Adventszeit) begins four Sundays before Christmas. Families light one more candle on the Advent wreath (der Adventskranz) each Sunday, and children open a door of the Advent calendar (der Adventskalender) each day from 1 to 24 December.
- Christmas markets (Weihnachtsmärkte) open in late November in almost every town. Famous ones are the Christkindlesmarkt in Nuremberg, the Striezelmarkt in Dresden (one of the oldest, since 1434) and the market at Cologne Cathedral. They sell Glühwein (mulled wine), gebrannte Mandeln (roasted sugared almonds), Lebkuchen (gingerbread) and handmade decorations.
- On 6 December (Nikolaustag) children put a boot outside the door and St. Nicholas fills it with sweets.
- The main celebration is on Christmas Eve, 24 December (Heiligabend). Families decorate the tree (der Weihnachtsbaum / Tannenbaum) and give presents in the evening. In many regions the presents are brought by the "Christkind" (Christ child), in others by the Weihnachtsmann (Father Christmas).
- 25 and 26 December are public holidays (erster und zweiter Weihnachtsfeiertag).
- Typical Christmas foods: Stollen (fruit bread from Dresden), Plätzchen (biscuits), roast goose (Weihnachtsgans).
- Greeting: Frohe Weihnachten! (Merry Christmas!)

## Carnival (Karneval / Fasching / Fastnacht)
- Called Karneval in the Rhineland (Cologne, Düsseldorf, Mainz), Fasching in Bavaria and Fastnacht in the southwest.
- The carnival season officially starts on 11 November at 11:11 a.m.
- The high point is the week before Ash Wednesday (Aschermittwoch) in February or March: Weiberfastnacht (Thursday, when women cut off men's ties), parades on Rosenmontag (Rose Monday) with floats and sweets thrown to the crowd.
- Cologne shouts "Kölle Alaaf!", Düsseldorf and Mainz shout "Helau!".

## Easter (Ostern)
- Good Friday (Karfreitag) and Easter Monday (Ostermontag) are public holidays.
- The Easter bunny (der Osterhase) hides painted eggs (Ostereier) in the garden for children.
- Families decorate branches with painted eggs (der Osterstrauch).

## German Unity Day (Tag der Deutschen Einheit) – 3 October
- National holiday celebrating the reunification of East and West Germany on 3 October 1990.

## Other holidays and traditions
- New Year's Eve (Silvester), 31 December: fireworks at midnight. Many Germans watch the short British comedy sketch "Dinner for One" on TV, and some pour melted metal or wax into water to predict the future (Bleigießen / Wachsgießen).
- Labour Day (Tag der Arbeit), 1 May: public holiday. In many villages people put up a decorated maypole (der Maibaum).
- Walpurgisnacht (30 April): night of the witches, celebrated in the Harz mountains.
- St. Martin's Day (Martinstag), 11 November: children walk through the streets with lanterns (Laternen) singing songs.
- Cannstatter Volksfest in Stuttgart is the second-largest beer festival after Oktoberfest.
- Many public holidays are different in each federal state (Bundesland). Bavaria has the most.
