# Everyday Life in Germany (Alltag in Deutschland)

## Punctuality (Pünktlichkeit)
- Being on time is very important. Arriving five minutes early for a meeting is normal; arriving late without telling anyone is considered rude.
- A German saying: "Fünf Minuten vor der Zeit ist des Soldaten Pünktlichkeit." (Five minutes early is a soldier's punctuality.)

## Recycling and the bottle deposit (Mülltrennung und Pfand)
- Germans separate their rubbish into different bins:
  - Gelbe Tonne / Gelber Sack (yellow) – plastic and metal packaging
  - Blaue Tonne (blue) – paper and cardboard
  - Biotonne (brown or green) – food and garden waste
  - Restmüll (grey or black) – everything else
  - Glass is taken to public glass containers, separated by colour: white, brown and green.
- Pfand is a deposit on bottles and cans. You pay extra when you buy a drink (usually 25 cents for single-use plastic bottles and cans, 8 or 15 cents for reusable glass bottles). You get the money back by returning the bottle to a machine (Pfandautomat) in a supermarket.

## Sunday rest (Sonntagsruhe)
- On Sundays and public holidays almost all shops are closed by law. Bakeries, petrol stations and shops in train stations are exceptions.
- Sunday is a quiet day (Ruhetag): people avoid loud activities like mowing the lawn, drilling or doing laundry in shared buildings.
- There is also a "night rest" (Nachtruhe) usually from 22:00 to 6:00 in residential buildings.

## Shopping and money
- Many Germans still like to pay in cash (Bargeld), although card and mobile payment are now common. Some small cafés and bakeries only accept cash ("Nur Barzahlung").
- Supermarkets: Aldi, Lidl, Rewe, Edeka. Drugstores: dm and Rossmann.
- Shoppers often pack their own bags quickly at the checkout; bring your own shopping bag (die Einkaufstasche) because plastic bags cost money.

## Social customs
- Greetings: people shake hands when meeting someone formally. Friends may hug.
- Formal vs informal "you": use "Sie" with strangers, older people, officials and at work until someone offers "du" (das Duzen). Young people and students usually use "du" with each other.
- Titles are important: "Herr Müller", "Frau Schmidt", "Herr Doktor Weber".
- When toasting, look the other person in the eyes and say "Prost!" or "Zum Wohl!".
- Germans are known for direct communication: they say clearly what they mean, which is considered honest rather than rude.
- Birthdays: it is considered bad luck to congratulate someone before their birthday. The person whose birthday it is often brings cake to work for colleagues.

## Food habits
- Breakfast often includes fresh bread rolls (Brötchen) from the bakery, with butter, jam, cheese or cold meats.
- Traditionally, lunch is the main warm meal, and dinner (Abendbrot) is cold: bread with toppings.
- Kaffee und Kuchen: coffee and cake in the afternoon, especially on Sundays.
- Germany has more than 3,000 types of bread; German bread culture is listed as intangible cultural heritage by the German UNESCO Commission.
- Beer is regulated by the Reinheitsgebot (Purity Law) of 1516, which originally allowed only water, barley and hops.

## Free time (Freizeit)
- Football (Fußball) is the most popular sport. The top league is the Bundesliga. Germany's men's team has won the World Cup four times (1954, 1974, 1990, 2014).
- Many people are members of clubs (Vereine): sports clubs, music clubs, gardening clubs. There is a saying that when three Germans meet, they found a club.
- Allotment gardens (Schrebergärten / Kleingärten) are small rented gardens in cities.
- Hiking (Wandern) and cycling are very popular.
- Germans have at least 20 days of paid holiday per year by law (for a 5-day week); most employees get 25–30 days.

## Healthcare and bureaucracy
- Health insurance (Krankenversicherung) is compulsory for everyone.
- When you move into a new flat, you must register your address at the local citizens' office (Bürgeramt) within two weeks. This is called "Anmeldung".
- Germany is known for paperwork and forms (das Formular). Many official letters still come by post.
