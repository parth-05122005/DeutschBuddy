# German History: Key Events (Deutsche Geschichte)

## Early history
- **9 AD** – Battle of the Teutoburg Forest: Germanic tribes led by Arminius (Hermann) defeated three Roman legions, stopping Rome's expansion east of the Rhine.
- **800** – Charlemagne (Karl der Große) was crowned emperor. His empire later developed into the Holy Roman Empire (Heiliges Römisches Reich), a loose union of hundreds of states that lasted until 1806.
- **around 1450** – Johannes Gutenberg in Mainz invented printing with movable metal type. The Gutenberg Bible (about 1455) was one of the first major books printed in Europe.
- **1517** – Martin Luther published his 95 Theses in Wittenberg, starting the Protestant Reformation. His translation of the Bible into German helped create a standard written German language.
- **1618–1648** – The Thirty Years' War devastated the German lands.

## Unification and empire
- **1871** – Germany was unified as the German Empire (Deutsches Kaiserreich) under Prussian leadership. Otto von Bismarck became the first Chancellor and Wilhelm I the Emperor (Kaiser). Bismarck introduced the world's first national health and pension insurance systems in the 1880s.
- **1914–1918** – World War I. Germany was defeated and the emperor abdicated.

## Weimar Republic and the Nazi dictatorship
- **1919–1933** – The Weimar Republic, Germany's first democracy. It suffered from hyperinflation (1923) and the Great Depression.
- **1933** – Adolf Hitler and the Nazi party (NSDAP) came to power and created a dictatorship.
- **1939–1945** – World War II, started by Germany's invasion of Poland. The Nazi regime murdered about six million Jews in the Holocaust (die Shoah), as well as Sinti and Roma, disabled people, political opponents and many others. Germany surrendered on 8 May 1945.
- Remembering these crimes (Erinnerungskultur, the culture of remembrance) is an important part of German politics and education today. Examples are the Memorial to the Murdered Jews of Europe in Berlin and the "Stolpersteine" – small brass plates in the pavement in front of the homes of victims.

## A divided country
- **1949** – Two German states were founded: the Federal Republic of Germany (BRD, West Germany, democratic, capital Bonn) and the German Democratic Republic (DDR, East Germany, a socialist state controlled by the Soviet Union, capital East Berlin).
- **1950s** – West Germany experienced fast economic growth called the "Wirtschaftswunder" (economic miracle).
- **13 August 1961** – East Germany built the Berlin Wall (die Berliner Mauer) to stop people escaping to the West. The wall was about 155 km long and surrounded West Berlin. At least 140 people were killed trying to cross it.
- **9 November 1989** – After peaceful mass protests in East Germany ("Wir sind das Volk!" – "We are the people!"), the Berlin Wall fell (der Mauerfall).
- **3 October 1990** – German reunification (die Wiedervereinigung). This date is now the national holiday, Tag der Deutschen Einheit.
- **1991** – The parliament decided to move the capital back to Berlin.

## Modern Germany
- Germany is a founding member of the European Union (and its predecessor in 1957) and uses the euro since 2002.
- Political system: a federal parliamentary republic with 16 federal states (Bundesländer). The parliament is the Bundestag. The head of government is the Federal Chancellor (Bundeskanzler); the head of state is the Federal President (Bundespräsident), whose role is mostly ceremonial.
- Angela Merkel was the first female Chancellor (2005–2021).
- The constitution is called the Basic Law (das Grundgesetz), adopted in 1949. Article 1 says: "Die Würde des Menschen ist unantastbar." (Human dignity is inviolable.)
