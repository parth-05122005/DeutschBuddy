# Famous Germans and German Inventions (Berühmte Deutsche und Erfindungen)

## Music
- **Johann Sebastian Bach** (1685–1750) – baroque composer from Eisenach; worked in Leipzig. Famous works: the Brandenburg Concertos and the St Matthew Passion.
- **Ludwig van Beethoven** (1770–1827) – born in Bonn. Composed nine symphonies; the "Ode to Joy" (Ode an die Freude) from his 9th Symphony is the anthem of the European Union. He continued composing after becoming deaf.
- **Johannes Brahms** (1833–1897) – composer from Hamburg, known for "Brahms' Lullaby" (Wiegenlied).
- **Richard Wagner** (1813–1883) – opera composer; the Bayreuth Festival still performs his operas every summer.
- Modern bands: Kraftwerk (pioneers of electronic music), Rammstein, Scorpions.

## Literature and philosophy
- **Johann Wolfgang von Goethe** (1749–1832) – Germany's most famous writer. Main work: "Faust". The Goethe-Institut, which teaches German worldwide, is named after him.
- **Friedrich Schiller** (1759–1805) – poet and playwright, friend of Goethe; wrote the poem "Ode an die Freude".
- **The Brothers Grimm** (Jacob and Wilhelm Grimm) – collected fairy tales like Hänsel und Gretel, Rotkäppchen (Little Red Riding Hood), Schneewittchen (Snow White) and Aschenputtel (Cinderella). They also started the first big German dictionary.
- **Immanuel Kant** (1724–1804), **Karl Marx** (1818–1883) and **Friedrich Nietzsche** (1844–1900) – influential philosophers.
- **Thomas Mann** (1875–1955) – Nobel Prize in Literature 1929; novel "Buddenbrooks".
- **Anne Frank** (1929–1945) – born in Frankfurt; her diary written while hiding from the Nazis in Amsterdam is read worldwide.

## Science
- **Albert Einstein** (1879–1955) – born in Ulm; theory of relativity (E = mc²); Nobel Prize in Physics 1921.
- **Wilhelm Conrad Röntgen** (1845–1923) – discovered X-rays in 1895 (in German still called "Röntgenstrahlen"); won the first Nobel Prize in Physics in 1901.
- **Max Planck** (1858–1947) – founder of quantum theory. The Max Planck Society is named after him.
- **Carl Friedrich Gauss** (1777–1855) – one of the greatest mathematicians ("prince of mathematicians").
- **Robert Koch** (1843–1910) – discovered the bacteria causing tuberculosis and cholera.

## Inventions from Germany
- **Printing press with movable type** – Johannes Gutenberg, around 1450.
- **Automobile** – Carl Benz built the first practical car with a petrol engine, the Benz Patent-Motorwagen, in 1885–1886. In 1888 his wife Bertha Benz made the first long-distance car journey (about 100 km from Mannheim to Pforzheim).
- **Diesel engine** – Rudolf Diesel, 1890s.
- **Computer** – Konrad Zuse built the Z3 in Berlin in 1941, the first working programmable, fully automatic digital computer.
- **MP3 audio format** – developed at the Fraunhofer Institute in Erlangen in the late 1980s and 1990s.
- **Aspirin** – first synthesised in a stable form at Bayer in 1897.
- **Chip card / smart card** – patented by Jürgen Dethloff and Helmut Gröttrup in 1968–69.
- **Gummy bears** – Hans Riegel of Bonn (Haribo = HAns RIegel BOnn) created them in 1922.
- **Christmas tree tradition, the jeans of Levi Strauss** (born in Buttenheim, Bavaria) and the **Fanta** drink (created in Germany in 1940) also have German roots.

## Famous German companies
Volkswagen, BMW, Mercedes-Benz, Porsche, Audi, Siemens, Bosch, SAP (Europe's largest software company), Adidas and Puma (founded by the brothers Adolf and Rudolf Dassler in Herzogenaurach), BASF, Bayer, Lufthansa, Deutsche Telekom.

## Sport
- **Franz Beckenbauer** – won the football World Cup as a player (1974) and as a coach (1990).
- **Michael Schumacher** – seven-time Formula 1 world champion.
- **Steffi Graf** – won 22 Grand Slam tennis titles and the "Golden Slam" in 1988.
- **Dirk Nowitzki** – NBA champion with the Dallas Mavericks in 2011.
